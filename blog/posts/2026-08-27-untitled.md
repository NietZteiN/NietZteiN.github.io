"Man only plays when in the full meaning of the word he is a man, and he is only completely a man when he plays."
Friedrich Schiller, Letters on the Aesthetic Education of Man

When I think about research from a meta perspective, I keep coming back to the idea of play.

A joke is basically an experiment. Incongruity theory describes humor as setting up an expectation and breaking it with a surprise, which is also what makes a pun work.

In a classroom, you learn a fixed series of steps. Research is different because it is about trying stuff out. My biggest worry used to be that I did not know enough to contribute. In machine learning, even high schoolers manage to publish at top conferences. You just have to test ideas, stay inventive, fail, and experiment. It works a lot like landing a joke.

If research is a kind of play or a language game, maybe an LLM could do it purely inside an embedding space. The real difference is that an LLM lacks phenomenal experience. World models might approximate that, but it is not the same as genuine experience. Schiller might argue that only humans combine rational thought with raw sensory drive.

This connects to the Kuleshov effect in film, where cutting between two unrelated images forces the viewer to find a new synthesis between them. Wittgenstein had a similar thought about taking a seed and planting it in foreign soil. That synthesis is really all interdisciplinary work is.
